# Rule editions: 1980/2013 US vs 2024 Canada

Two editions of the *same* Rule of the Third Order of the SSPX, both signed by Archbishop
Marcel Lefebvre. They are not identical, and the differences land exactly on the parts an
app has to automate — which days you fast, and what satisfies the daily obligation.

- **US District** — *Sursum Corda* No. 4, Winter 2013, reprinting the Rule as promulgated
  **November 1, 1980**. This is the edition the user is professed under. **Normative.**
- **Canada District** — *Third Order Handbook*, © 2024, Christ the King Priory, Langley BC.
  The same Rule with pastoral updates, plus the full practical apparatus (Prime, Compline,
  a meditation method, alternate morning/evening prayers, the Formula for Reception of
  Postulants, the Profession ceremony, and an Examination of Conscience keyed to the Rule).

## Identical in both

Purpose; Patron; Relationship to the Society; eligibility (all Catholics, lay or priest;
children from twelve with parents' permission); emblems (the medal of St. Pius X and a
Cross); the Spirit of the Third Order; the three steps of membership (enrollment →
one-year postulancy → profession); the daily Rosary; weekly attendance at the Immemorial
Mass and explicitly not the Novus Ordo; Penance every two weeks or at least monthly; a
retreat every two years; the recommended reading list; abstinence on all Fridays;
abstaining from television and unclean reading and practising sobriety; the obligations of
the married; professional and social duties; the Organization section.

## Where they differ

| | **1980 / 2013 US** (yours) | **2024 Canada** | Consequence for the app |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily, in place of Mass | "or **fifteen minutes of meditation**" | "or **a quarter hour of prayer**" | Same fifteen minutes, but yours names *meditation* — a specific discipline, not prayer generally. Yours is the more precise and the more demanding. The app should schedule mental prayer, not a generic timer. |
| Fast days | "Ember Days, on **Vigils of great feasts**, on Ash Wednesday, and **Holy Saturday**" | "a) Ember Days; b) the vigils of **Pentecost, All Saints (Oct 31), and Christmas (Dec 24)**; c) Ash Wednesday, and **Good Friday**" | Two real divergences. See below. |
| Priests' extra fasts | *absent* | "Society priests also keep all Fridays in Lent and the vigil of the Immaculate Conception (Dec 7)" | Not applicable to a layman; model it as a `priest`-only item. |
| Abstinence | "on **all Fridays**" | "on **Ash Wednesday and all Fridays**", also *recommended* on every fast day above | Yours: Fridays year-round. Ash Wednesday abstinence binds under general law anyway. |
| Mortification list | television, unclean reading, sobriety | adds "**mortify one's use of the internet and all electronic media**" | The 2024 addition is the same spirit updated for 2024. Offer it as an opt-in counsel, clearly labelled as not from your edition. |
| Married | avoid "television, unclean magazines" | adds "free and unrestricted access to the internet" | Same pattern. Opt-in. |
| Apparatus | Rule text only (plus Lefebvre's early-1980s articles on the spirit of the Society) | Rule + Prime + Compline + meditation method + alternate prayers + reception formula + profession ceremony + **examination of conscience keyed to the Rule** | The Canadian handbook is where the app's prayer content and examen structure come from. |

### The two fasting divergences, in detail

**1. Holy Saturday (yours) vs Good Friday (2024).** Yours names Holy Saturday, reflecting
the older discipline — Good Friday's fast bound everyone under general law, so the Rule
named the *additional* day. The 2024 edition names Good Friday instead. Practically: keep
both. Good Friday is not optional for any Catholic, and your edition asks for Holy
Saturday on top.

**2. "Vigils of great feasts" is unenumerated in your edition.** This is the single place
where your text cannot be mechanised without a decision, and the app must *ask* rather
than guess. The traditional vigils with a fast were Christmas, Pentecost, the Assumption,
All Saints, and historically the Immaculate Conception. The 2024 edition enumerates three
(Pentecost, All Saints, Christmas) and omits the Assumption. The onboarding presents the
traditional list with the 2024 selection pre-checked, shows the user both texts, and lets
them confirm with their chaplain.

## Verdict

Your instinct is right, with one qualification. **The 2013 US text is better as the
*norm*** — it is the original 1980 promulgation, it is what you professed, and its wording
("meditation") is more exact. **The 2024 handbook is better as the *apparatus*** — it is
where Prime, Compline, the meditation method, and the examen actually live.

So the app treats them as what they are: one Rule, two editions.

- **Normative edition:** 1980/2013 US. Generates your obligations.
- **Reference edition:** 2024 CA. Supplies prayer texts, the examen, and *clearly labelled*
  optional counsels (internet mortification) and interpretations (which vigils).
- Never silently merged. Any item not from your edition is marked, and the app can show
  you the two texts side by side.

This is why `Rule` carries an `edition` and the app supports a **diff view between
editions**. No prayer app has ever had a "which edition of your rule are you under"
concept, and the last twenty minutes proved it is needed.
